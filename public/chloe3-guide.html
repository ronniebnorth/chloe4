Chapter 1
What is Chloe?

A musical scale is a set of notes chosen from the 12 semitones of the chromatic scale. Western music mostly uses two of them — the major scale and the minor scale — but there are thousands of others, each with its own mood, colour, and cultural history.

Chloe generates every mathematically valid scale from 3 to 8 notes and lets you hear them all. It filters out scales with three consecutive semitones (which tend to sound like chromatic noise rather than a scale), leaving a catalogue of musically interesting structures, including many you'll recognise and many you won't have encountered before.

The core idea: Drone a root note and step through a scale. Listen to what makes Lydian sound dreamy, Phrygian sound tense, a Japanese pentatonic sound sparse, or an octatonic diminished scale sound like a film score. Chloe makes that exploration immediate and tactile.

Chloe4 goes past the 12 semitones. Every scale is stored as its pitches in cents (a semitone is 100 cents), so the catalogue also holds Arabic maqam and Persian dastgah with their quarter tones, Turkish makam in 53 commas per octave, Byzantine chant in 72 moria, and exact tunings such as just intonation and the Chinese pentatonic in pure fifths. All of Chloe3's 12-note scales are still here, unchanged. Chapter 7 covers the world scales.

Almost all sound is synthesised live in your browser using the Web Audio API, with no plugins. The exceptions are the orchestra and the sampled choir voices, which download real recordings the first time you switch them on.

1.1
The scale catalogue

Scales are organised into families. A family is a group of scales that share the same interval structure — they're rotations of each other, the way C major and D Dorian are both rotations of the same seven-note pattern. Each rotation is called a mode.

Within a family, modes are sorted from brightest to darkest. Brightness is calculated by summing the semitone positions of the active notes — more sharps means brighter. This naturally produces the familiar Lydian → Ionian → Mixolydian → Dorian → Aeolian → Phrygian → Locrian ordering for the seven standard modes.

Families are named by note count and a number: hep-6 means the 6th heptatonic (7-note) family, which is the standard diatonic family containing all the church modes.

Chapter 2
Binary Notation

Every scale in Chloe is represented as a 12-bit binary string — one bit for each semitone of the chromatic scale, starting from the root note. A 1 means that semitone is active in the scale; a 0 means it's skipped.

Reading from left to right: bit 0 is the root (always 1), bit 1 is one semitone up, bit 2 is a whole tone, and so on up to bit 11 (a major seventh).

The binary string is 101011010101, and its value is 2741. (Chloe writes the root on the left so the string reads like the scale. Standard binary writes the root on the right, as 101010110101, so a binary calculator will only give 2741 for that reversed form. The number is the same either way.) This number is a compact, unique fingerprint for each scale — independent of root note or key, since it describes the interval pattern, not the specific pitches.

In Chloe4 the 12-bit codes still work exactly as above. Quarter-tone scales use a 24-bit code, one bit per quarter tone, written root-first the same way. Scales in commas, moria or exact cents don't fit any grid, so Chloe stores their pitches in cents and gives each one a stable ID made from those pitches.
2.1
The scale wheel

Chloe displays each selected scale as a polygon on a circle: 24 points arranged clockwise, one per quarter tone (every other point is a piano note). A note in commas, moria or cents sits at its true angle between them. Active scale tones are highlighted in amber and connected by lines.

The shape of the polygon gives you an immediate visual feel for the scale's character:

Sparse scales
Wide polygons
Pentatonics produce a loose, open shape — five widely spaced points. Easy to navigate, forgiving, a lot of air between notes.
Dense scales
Compact polygons
Octatonic and chromatic-heavy scales produce tight, almost circular shapes. More tension, more colour, less obvious resolution.

The root note is always at the top. Rotating the root (using the key selector) rotates the whole polygon — the shape stays the same because the interval structure doesn't change.

2.3
Audio Visualiser

The centre panel shows a real-time circular waveform that responds to every note played. The waveform ring pulses outward with amplitude and its colour smoothly tracks the chromatic pitch of the current note — each of the 12 pitch classes has a distinct hue, lerping to the new colour each time a note changes. A faint outer ring shows the full chromatic colour wheel for reference.

2.4
Scale Info Panel

The two tabs above the centre panel (◎ visualizer / ℹ info) switch between the visualiser and a scale information view. The info panel always shows the scale's notes, interval pattern, and decimal value. For named scales, if you have entered a Demo API key, it fetches a short AI-generated guide from Claude covering the scale's mood and character, its origins, and notable songs or genres that use it. Results are cached for the session so switching tabs is instant after the first load.

Chapter 3
Playback

Chloe's playback is designed to let you hear a scale in context rather than just reading its notes. There are several layers you can combine freely.

3.1
Drone

The drone sustains the root note continuously while you explore the scale above it. This is the most direct way to hear the modal character of a scale — each note in the arpeggio or melody is heard against a fixed tonal centre, making the tensions and resolutions audible rather than theoretical.

The DRONE OCT control drops the drone 1, 2, or 3 octaves below the playing register, giving it weight as a bass foundation. The DRONE VOL slider sets its level independently of the notes.

DRONE TYPE sets its sound: sine, organ, pad, strings, tanpura, throat singing, jaw harp, juno (an 80s polysynth pad), dam (a second duduk), chant (monks on a low om), ison (the low voices of a Byzantine choir, each breathing at his own time so it never stops), didgeridoo, or white, pink and brown noise.

3.2
Arpeggio and Melody

The Play button starts stepping through the scale. It has two modes:

Mode
Arpeggio
Steps through scale notes in order — ascending, descending, or random. Predictable and pattern-focused. Direction is set by the arrow controls.
Mode
Melody
A weighted random walk — favours stepwise motion but occasionally leaps an octave or reverses direction. More organic and phrase-like.
3.3

The RHYTHM control sets the note-spacing pattern for arpeggio mode. All rhythms are BPM-synced at eighth-note resolution.

Instead of single notes, the arpeggio can play chords built from the current scale at each step. Available voicings:

3.5
Beat

The Beat button adds a percussion track — kick, snare, and hi-hat — synthesised live in the browser (no samples). It runs independently of the arpeggio so you can combine them freely.

The beat automatically follows the RHYTHM setting: Even gives a straight rock beat, Swing adds triplet groove, Gallop drives a double-kick pattern, Waltz plays in 3/4, and Clave runs the son clave rhythm at 16th-note resolution. BPM changes take effect on the next step.

Chapter 4
Sound

The instruments are synthesised in real time using the Web Audio API, built from oscillators, noise, filters and envelopes in JavaScript. The choir and oohs voices and the orchestra use recorded samples instead. A journey or world can choose its own instrument; the instrument row then shows the one playing with a dashed outline. The four below are the originals; the full list follows.

Piano

The beat track runs dry by design — it bypasses reverb and delay so the kick and snare stay punchy and present while the melodic layer can be as wet and spacious as you like.
4.2
Timbre

For the piano, guitar, and xylo instruments, the TIMBRE control shifts the oscillator waveform between sine, triangle, sawtooth, and square — from warm and smooth to bright and buzzy. Less relevant for guitar (which uses noise synthesis) and disabled for the space instrument, which has its own fixed character.

Chapter 5
Auto & Demo Modes
5.1
Auto mode

The Auto button makes Chloe explore autonomously, moving from scale to scale and changing root note, rhythm, arpeggio direction, chord voicing, BPM, sound and effects as it goes. The ⚙ panel lets you choose which of those Auto may change. A journey (Chapter 6) gives Auto a destination instead of a free wander.

This is useful as a background listening mode, or as a way to quickly survey scales you haven't spent time with. The current scale and settings appear in the status banner.

5.2
Demo mode — Claude AI

The Claude button enables AI-driven exploration. This uses the Claude Haiku model via the Anthropic API to make musical choices — not just randomising, but actively thinking about contrast, variety, and musical interest as it moves between scales.

Each turn, Claude receives the current state (scale, root, rhythm, BPM), the full history of scales played this session (up to 20), and the full catalogue of named scales with their interval patterns. The history lets it contrast musically — varying brightness, note density, and feel, and avoiding scales it has recently explored. It returns a JSON choice including a short piece of commentary on the scale's character, which appears in the banner.

★ DEMO Lydian Dominant — a Mixolydian with a raised fourth, sitting
between brightness and blues tension. Overtone series in miniature.
           ✦ It would be interesting to hear microtonal tuning variants of these scales.

Claude can also leave feature requests — if it notices something missing or imagines a capability the app doesn't have, it can write a brief note in amber alongside its commentary. These requests accumulate in the demo log so you can review what it's been asking for over a session.

API key required. Click ★ Claude and enter an Anthropic API key (available at console.anthropic.com under API Keys — this requires separate billing from Claude.ai subscriptions). Your key is stored in your browser's local storage only and is never sent anywhere except directly to the Anthropic API.
5.3
The demo log

Both Auto and Demo modes maintain a log of recent scale choices, accessible via the ▾ log button in the banner. Each entry shows the scale name, root note, BPM, rhythm, and commentary. Clicking any entry replays that exact combination. The ⎘ copy button exports the full log as plain text to your clipboard — useful for noting down interesting scales Claude discovered during a session.

Chapter 6
Journeys & Worlds
6.1
Journeys

A journey is a shape over time: 5, 15, 30 or 60 minutes (LENGTH). Choosing one starts Auto, which then moves scales, tempo, reverb and note length along that shape. On the Listen page you can draw your own.

JourneyShape
dawn, dusk, descentDark to bright, bright to dark, and down and back up

waitA pulse through suspended scales, a long hold without a 3rd, then the 3rd arrives
endless, mapA slow calm wander that never arrives; a shuffle for measuring with a headband
drawYour own line: high for bright, low for dark; a scales row limits it to one tradition
persiaA tour of the Persian dastgahs, Shur up to Mahur and home
shurInside one dastgah: the notes stay, the home note climbs (Abu Ata, Bayat-e Tork, Afshari, Dashti) and comes home

byzantineChant in 72 moria, chant voice and choir taking turns over the ison
spiralThe Chinese pentatonic climbing a pure fifth at a time, drifting by the Pythagorean comma (see 7.4)

The world journeys lean briefly into related scales about once a minute and come back, the way a taksim or a radif performance moves.

6.2
Worlds and the orchestra

A world brings a lead instrument, a rhythm, a drone and surroundings that belong together: steppe, desert, ocean, jungle, thunderstorm, cave, temple, arctic, mountain, carnival, celtic, night drive, fire circle and new wave. WANDER drifts from world to world.

SYMPHONIC (orchestra on the Listen page) adds a sampled orchestra under the line: strings on chords from the current scale, cellos, double bass, timpani, violin and flute runs, and a choir toward the epic end of MOOD. Its WANDER lets it come and go on its own.

Chapter 7
Beyond 12 Notes
7.1
World scales and how they are written
TraditionSteps per octaveWritten as
Arabic maqam, Persian dastgah24 (quarter tones, 50 cents)E½♭ (half-flat), C½♯ (half-sharp)
Turkish makam53 commas (about 22.6 cents)B⁻¹ = B one comma flat
Byzantine chant72 moria (16.7 cents)intervals in moria, e.g. 12·10·8
Real intonationexact centsE−14¢ = 14 cents below E

These intervals are intended, not out of tune: Rast's 3rd sits between major and minor, Bayati's 2nd between a half and a whole step. Brightness is worked out the same way as for 12-note scales, from the exact positions, so world scales fall into place on the same bright-to-dark ladder.

7.2
Key in cents

A journey can move the home note between the piano's notes: Shur's Abu Ata sits on a half-flat, the makam journey's Dügah sits 9 commas (203.8 cents) above Rast, and the spiral drifts by fractions of a cent. The root then shows it (D½♭, F+22¢) and everything follows, drone, orchestra and drums included. Stopping the journey puts the key back on the semitones.

7.3
Resolution

RESOLUTION (Studio, under TUNING) rounds every pitch to the nearest of that many equal steps per octave when it plays: 12 is the piano, 24 quarter tones, 53 Turkish commas, 72 Byzantine moria, 1200 exact. The world journeys raise it to what they need. The scale list and brightness always use the exact pitches.

7.4
The spiral of fifths

Stack pure 3:2 fifths and fold them into one octave, and they never quite close the circle: 12 fifths miss by 23.5 cents (the Pythagorean comma), 53 miss by only 3.6. Chinese theorists worked through this long ago: Jing Fang carried the chain to 60 fifths in the 1st century BC, and Qian Lezhi to 360, one for each day of the year, in the 5th century AD.

The spiral journey plays a Chinese pentatonic in pure fifths and moves its home note up a pure fifth at a time, as in xuangong ("rotating the gong"). After 12 moves it is back on its first note but 23.5 cents sharp; its 53 moves end 3.6 cents from home. Then it walks on along Qian Lezhi's chain toward 360 until you stop it. Qian's 360 pitches are only 1.8 to 3.6 cents apart, too close to hear as separate notes, which is why the spiral walks the chain instead of playing it as one scale.

Chapter 9
Sharing & URL State

Every setting in Chloe is encoded into the URL. The ⬡ share button (top right) builds a URL encoding the current scale, root note, instrument, BPM, tuning, rhythm, chord voicing, arpeggio direction, reverb, delay, and sidebar width — then copies it to your clipboard.

Paste or bookmark the URL to restore the exact session later, or send it to someone else so they hear exactly what you were hearing. The URL is human-readable: each parameter has a short key, and the values are compact enough that the full URL stays shareable.

https://ronniebnorth.github.io/chloe4/?
jo=byzantine&jm=30&i=chant&dr=1&dw=ison&edo=72
